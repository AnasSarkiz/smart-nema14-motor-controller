## Bootstrap allocation and sequencing

Until a qualified PD contract, limit total 3.3 V logic demand to 150 mA, keep ENN high and CAN RS high, omit the encoder, and export no logic supply on either signal connector. This allocation exceeds the ST MCU all-peripherals-disabled 64 MHz characterization (9.9 mA max at 130 °C); peripheral/I/O demand must remain within the allocation. TI specifies CAN standby at 0.6 mA max, TMUX1511 at 70 µA max, TLV803E at about 1 µA max, and TMC2209 disabled VM current at 10 mA max under its stated clock/test conditions. Separate engineering allowance covers those VM/control loads. LED/pull-up loads are included in the allocation. External signals must be 3.3 V logic or dry-contact limits, with no back-powered load or power export.

Using 3.465 V output, 4.2 V VM, a conservative 60% efficiency allowance, 10 mA disabled driver, 5 mA additional control allowance and 28.48 mA bulk charging gives 0.24973 A screened bootstrap demand, leaving 0.16694 A below the 0.41667 A low current-limit screen. The source sees at most 0.59167 A screened eFuse/control load versus its required ≥1.5 A advertisement. The converter has no guaranteed minimum efficiency or soft-start duration; this engineering calculation does not establish cold-start waveforms. Actual start, brownout and PD-contract transitions remain Stage 7 measurements. Normal post-contract logic allocation remains ≤0.3 A.

The paper schematic/BOM review can proceed to routing for this bounded programmable prototype. Physical PD negotiation and contract-aware protection firmware are not delivered. Keep the hardware-default motor-disable/CAN-standby states until firmware validates the actual source, current, rail voltage and fault inputs. Firmware must hold ENN high on disconnect/fault and configure the motor before motion. High-frequency transient and temperature measurements remain explicit prototype tests after the actual copper layout is reviewed.
